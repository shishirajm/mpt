Mac Precitec India — five website directions
at desktop, iPad and mobile

Five complete homepage designs for macprecitecindia.com, benchmarked against Kennametal (dense catalogue and commerce utility) and MAPAL (editorial, sector-led brand storytelling).

The strategic read: Kennametal sells thousands of catalogue SKUs online. MAPAL sells engineering partnership. MPI is closer to MAPAL — a 55-person Bangalore specialist whose product is custom multi-step tooling and turnkey proving-out. So every direction converts an enquiry with a component drawing, not an add-to-cart.

What's new in this version. Each design is now genuinely responsive rather than a scaled-down picture: the layouts reflow using CSS container queries, so the iPad frame (834 px) and phone frame (390 px) show the real breakpoint behaviour — collapsed navigation, restacked grids, retuned type scales. Mobile-specific patterns have been added throughout: hamburger navigation, a sticky bottom action bar on every concept, horizontally scrolling tab and menu rails, and in Direction 03 the spec table converts to stacked product cards rather than shrinking to nothing.

What changed: the raster logo is rebuilt as clean vector geometry — the circle is truly concentric, the pyramid apex sits on the vertical axis, and the base band gets a proper taper so it reads as a machined plinth rather than a bar. Stroke weights are unified to a 6/5/5 hierarchy so the mark holds at 16 px favicon size and at laser-etch size on a tool body. The "MPI" letterforms carry a knockout halo, which keeps them legible where they cross the pyramid — the biggest legibility failure in the current version. Where I'd take this

  • Strongest single bet: Direction 05 (Turnkey). MPI's sales motion is "send us a drawing, we'll engineer the tool." Putting a working tool finder in the hero makes the site do the first sales conversation instead of describing it — and on mobile it's the only direction where the primary action is thumb-reachable in the first screen.
  • Best hybrid: 05's hero on 03's architecture. Tool finder and proposal flow from Turnkey, sitting on Tool Room's information architecture — mega menu, part codes, datasheets, persistent enquiry list. Credible to a purchase engineer and converting for a first-time visitor.
  • Direction 01 (Micron) is the safest choice if the priority is standing next to Kennametal and looking like a metrology-grade supplier. ±3 µm is a genuinely strong claim and no other direction uses it harder.
  • Direction 04 (Peenya) is the pick if the audience includes recruits, Indian OEM procurement or import-substitution buyers. Weakest for a global aerospace buyer, strongest for domestic trust.

Mobile decisions worth noting

  • A sticky action bar on every concept. Indian industrial buyers browse heavily on phones; the CTA should never be more than a thumb away. Each concept's bar carries a different promise — "quote in 2 working days", "3 tools in your enquiry", "proposal in 48 hrs".
  • Tables don't shrink, they transform. In Direction 03 the spec table swaps to stacked cards below 560 px rather than becoming a horizontal-scroll trap. On iPad it stays a table with a scroll rail, which is the right call at 834 px.
  • iPad is a real breakpoint, not a shrunk desktop. At 834 px the six-column sector grids drop to three, hero splits stack, and the 88 px display type in Direction 02 comes down to 62 px so the headline still lands in one or two lines instead of four.
  • Tap targets and rails. Sector tabs, top navigation and logo rows become horizontally scrolling rails instead of wrapping into three ragged lines, and every button clears 44 px of height.

Still needed before build

  • Real photography — the brochure's shop floor, gauging room and team shots are good and badly underused. This is the single biggest quality gap between MPI and MAPAL, and it isn't a layout problem.
  • Six to eight genuine application stories with a named component and a before/after cycle time.
  • Downloadable datasheets per tool family, and a decision on whether Hindi or Kannada locales are in scope — the type stacks chosen here all have Devanagari-capable fallbacks.
